The whole thing in four sentences

A teen who already knows GameChanger builds a free profile and picks the leagues they will drive to. A coach who needs a scorekeeper joins free, then requests a helper for a specific game. You look at both lists and pick the match. The software emails both sides each other's name, phone, and payment app, they text each other, the helper shows up, the coach pays them directly, and three hours after first pitch both get a review link.

The only manual step in the entire business is step 3: you choosing who to match. Everything before and after is automatic. That is the job you are protecting until you have enough volume to automate it.

What is never public

A helper's public profile page shows their name, skill, leagues, sports, games scored, founding number, star rating, and review text. That is it.

  • Age is stored but never displayed
  • Phone is only released at match time
  • Email is never displayed
  • Payment handle is only released at match time
  • Parent name and email are never displayed

Where it breaks as you grow. Matching by hand is the bottleneck and also the quality. At 5 games a week it is nothing. At 50 it is a part time job. The trigger to automate matching is when you are matching more than roughly 20 games a week, not before. Until then the hand match is what makes coaches trust a brand new service.
Two things still open before you scale. First, the Florida attorney on minors, parental consent, and contractor status. Second, no coach has been asked to pay anything yet, so willingness to pay is still a guess. Twenty completed matches answers the second one.
